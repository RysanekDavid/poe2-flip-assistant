**Patch stamp:** 0.5.0 "Return of the Ancients" launched 2026-05-29, hotfixes through 0.5.4. The prior league, "Fate of the Vaal" (0.4.0, launched 2025-12-12), had its league mechanic (Vaal Cultivation Orb, corrupted-unique manipulation) go core in 0.5. Data below is dated where sources gave a date; undated current-state snapshots are as of **2026-07-14** (league ~7 weeks old at time of writing).

## 1. Inflation structure

- **0.5.0 baseline shift:** patch 0.5 raised Divine Orb drop rates relative to 0.4, flattening the early-league curve — expect roughly a 10–20% Divine premium in the first days of a 0.5.x league vs. mid-league. [single-source — timesaver.gg, https://timesaver.gg/blog/poe2-mirror-of-kalandra-guide]
- **Week 1 → Week 3 Divine cost (in Chaos):** ~250–270 Chaos in week 1, dropping to 220–240 Chaos by week 3 as Chaos supply catches up. [single-source — Switchblade Gaming, https://www.switchbladegaming.com/path-of-exile-2/economy-guide-4/]
- **Exalt→Divine inflation, measured over a 36-day 0.5 window:** Divine Orb price rose from **78.4 Exalted → 598 Exalted**, i.e. Exalted Orbs lost roughly 87% of their value against Divine over that stretch — because Exalted is farmed/dropped at much higher volume than Divine. [single-source — Dads of Exile economy report, https://dadsofexile.com/economy-report]
- **Current ratio snapshot (2026-07-14):** 1 Divine ≈ **455 Exalted Orbs** ≈ 7.46 Chaos Orbs. [single-source — timesaver.gg, https://timesaver.gg/blog/poe2-exalted-orb-price-guide]
- **Mirror of Kalandra trajectory:** direction is confirmed by multiple independent sources (steep appreciation over the league), but exact figures diverge by snapshot date/source:
  - Dads of Exile: Mirror gained **+397% real value** (i.e., priced in Divine, not nominal) over 36 days, reaching **~6.5k Divine**. Framed as scarcity-driven appreciation, not generic inflation — Mirror doesn't get farmed, so its Divine-price rises as the Divine supply itself grows. [single-source, dated ~36 days into 0.5 — https://dadsofexile.com/economy-report]

## 2. Mirror service meta

- **Fee model observed (0.5-era, OpenMirror aggregator):** mirror services in 0.5 largely operate on a **flat Divine-Orb fee** (not a percentage cut) charged on top of the buyer supplying the Mirror itself ("fee + 1 mirror to copy the item"). Listed fees clustered **560–760 Divine**, topping out around **880 Divine** for the highest-demand items (e.g. a "Skull Song" Lunar Quarterstaff at 880 Div). Commonly mirrored categories: bows, wands, spears, crossbows, staves, body armor, helmets, rings, amulets — i.e., whatever the current meta's BiS mirror-tier crafted rare is. [single-source — OpenMirror archive, https://openmirror.shop/archived/poe2]
- **Historical Mirror-market pricing context (0.3, official forum, NOT 0.5 but shows the pattern):** community discussion pegged Mirror at "well over 1,000 Divine" after ~2 months in 0.3, with one commenter correcting a poster's "2,000 Divine" claim down to "1,300–1,400." Debate centered on whether this represented unfair inflation (exploitable mechanics) vs. normal end-of-league currency deflation. [CONFIRMED as a real forum debate, single league (0.3) — official PoE forum, https://www.pathofexile.com/forum/view-thread/3899561]
- **Practical mechanic:** buyer supplies (or pays for) the Mirror; the crafter supplies the flawless base item and the crafting-service fee; right-click Mirror → left-click target rare to produce a perfect permanent duplicate. This is the standard Mirror-of-Kalandra interaction, unchanged in 0.5. [CONFIRMED, standard mechanic — timesaver.gg + generic wiki description]
- **[Unverified]** Whether 0.5 mirror-service fees are commonly quoted as a percentage of item value anywhere in the community (as opposed to the flat-fee model observed above) could not be confirmed from available sources — no percentage-fee discussion surfaced in search.

## 4. Profit-crafting economics rules

- **Core methodology observed across creator content:** price-check the *finished, listed* item on poe.ninja/trade before committing currency, then back-calculate: (cost of base item) + (cost of all currency/omens/essences consumed across attempts, including bricked attempts) must be meaningfully below the going rate for a comparable finished item, because by the time a viewer replicates a video's craft, supply of that exact craft has increased and price has typically already started drooping.
- **Market saturation after popular creator videos:** the general dynamic is widely acknowledged in the community (a popular "easy profit craft" video causes many viewers to run the same recipe, flooding listings and compressing the margin within days), but a specific, sourced 0.5 example of a documented price crash tied to a named video could not be found in available search results. One relevant signal: a video titled "CRAFTING Is a TRAP in POE2 – DO THIS INSTEAD" argues that crafting *looks* strong on paper in a given patch but is a trap for most players because the margin evaporates once a method becomes common knowledge — but this was found for 0.4, not 0.5, and the mechanism described (broadcast → copy → saturate → margin compression) is the same pattern the task brief was asking about. [single-source, wrong patch (0.4) — YouTube, https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EHCzXZyLIHs] **[unverified for a specific 0.5 case]**.
- **Mirror-tier crafting cost reality (Fubgun-adjacent content, ice shot / bow crafts):** the *last* few percent of optimization on a mirror-tier bow reportedly costs more than every prior step combined — one documented process spent roughly **680 Divine total**, of which **~500 Divine** went to Exalted Orbs and Orbs of Light in the final push; other estimates for a comparable final-prefix push cite **40–50 Perfect Chaos Orbs + 10–12 "Sinestral Erasers."** A single misstep at this stage can brick the item and waste the entire investment. [single-source aggregation across mmogah/mmojugg/vortexgaming guide summaries of Fubgun-style content — could not fetch Fubgun's own video transcript directly; **[single-source, unverified exact figures]**]

### The Rathpith Globe (unique Sacred Focus) — THE mechanic question, resolved
**Short answer: it's a plain Divine Orb reroll. There is no special gambling mechanic, no Vaal-Orb requirement, and no "Fluxes of Aldur" involvement for the item's core variable rolls.**

- Rathpith Globe has exactly two *variable-range* affixes: **"(60–100)% increased Energy Shield"** and **"+(60–100) to maximum Life."** Its other three modifiers ("Non-Channelling Spells cost an additional 6% of max Life"; crit-chance-per-100-life; damage-per-100-life) are fixed-value, not ranged. [CONFIRMED across 2 independent sources — poe2db.tw fetch and Game8, https://game8.co/games/Path-of-Exile-2/archives/513357]
- Rerolling the two ranged affixes (ES% and flat Life) to a better roll within their existing (60–100) band uses a **standard Divine Orb** — the exact same generic mechanic every other item with ranged affixes in the game uses. There is no dedicated Rathpith-specific gambling currency, no requirement to corrupt it first, and no interaction with the 0.5 "Fluxes" mechanic (Fluxes only convert one *resistance element* into another on eligible items — they have no relevance to Rathpith Globe, which has no resistance mods). [CONFIRMED — poe2db.tw + Game8, both independently describing "updated using a Divine Orb" for the variable mods]
- **Separate, unrelated mechanic that got conflated in some search results:** the **Vaal Cultivation Orb** is a general endgame currency (obtained from Tier-3+ Sacrificial Chamber rooms in Atziri's Temple, or Royal Trove drops) that can (a) overwrite 1–2 modifiers on an already-*corrupted* Vaal-tagged unique with a random "cultivated" modifier from a large unique-specific pool (~185 possibilities cited), or (b) when used on a non-Vaal unique, convert it into a corrupted unique *of the same item class* (e.g., turning a belt into a random corrupted belt unique, potentially even Headhunter). This orb is **not part of Rathpith Globe's normal reroll loop** — it's a high-risk, low-control endgame gamble tool that happens to also be usable (as a bench-equivalent option) on a *corrupted* Rathpith Globe if a player wants to gamble away its guaranteed identity for a chance at something else entirely, which is a fundamentally different action than "rerolling Rathpith's numbers." [CONFIRMED as a real, distinct mechanic — poe2wiki.net/Vaal_Cultivation_Orb, Game8, ssegold guide, mmojugg guide — multiple independent corroborating sources]
- **Patch-history wrinkle relevant to the reroll decision:** 0.4.0 nerfed the crit-chance-per-100-life modifier from 5%→3% and the damage-per-100-life modifier from 10%→6%. Pre-nerf (legacy) Rathpith Globes retain the old, stronger fixed values *until* a Divine Orb is used on them, at which point they get updated to current (weaker) values — meaning **Divine-rerolling a legacy Rathpith Globe is a trap**: you're not just gambling on the ES%/Life roll, you're also permanently downgrading the fixed crit/damage modifiers to the post-nerf numbers. [CONFIRMED across 2 sources — poe2db.tw fetch and Game8]
- **Brick/EV economics of the reroll:** each Divine Orb reroll re-rolls *both* ranged affixes simultaneously (standard Divine Orb behavior — it rerolls all values on the item, not one at a time), so hitting a top roll on both ES% and flat Life at once is a compounded low-probability event; no source gave an exact hit-rate percentage or expected-Divine-to-perfect-roll figure for Rathpith Globe specifically. **[unverified — no EV/brick-rate data found]**.

## Wallet warnings

- **Don't Divine-reroll a pre-0.4.0 legacy Rathpith Globe expecting a pure upgrade** — it silently reverts the fixed crit%/damage% modifiers to the current (nerfed) values, permanently losing the legacy bonus even if the ES%/Life roll improves. [see §5]
- **"Fluxes" only touch resistance-type mods** — don't buy/farm Fluxes expecting them to help with non-resistance ranged affixes (e.g., Rathpith Globe's ES%/Life, or generic damage rolls); they exist purely to convert one elemental resistance type into another on eligible items. [single-source mechanic description — u4n.com, https://www.u4n.com/news/how-to-get-and-use-resistance-fluxes-in-poe-2.html]
- **Vaal Cultivation Orb is a gamble that can destroy a known-good unique's identity entirely**, not a controlled upgrade path — using it on a non-Vaal unique converts it into a random corrupted unique *of the same item class*, which could be worse, unsellable, or (rarely) a jackpot like Headhunter. Don't use it on an item you're not prepared to lose outright.
- **Creator-quoted Div/hour numbers are marketing ceilings**, not medians — none of the sourced guides disclose sample size, luck variance, or the Atlas/gear investment required to reach the quoted rate (§1 table). Budget for well below the headline number, especially in the first week before your Atlas tree is optimized.
- **Divine-rerolling anything late in crafting is where money actually disappears** — per the Fubgun-adjacent bow-crafting cost breakdown, the *final* optimization push (Divine-orb spam on the last prefix/suffix) reportedly costs more than every earlier crafting step combined, and a single misstep can brick the whole item. Budget the finishing step separately and larger than intuition suggests.
- **Popular "easy profit craft" videos saturate fast** — treat any specific recipe named in a high-view-count creator video as having a shrinking margin window measured in days, not weeks, once it's public. No hard 0.5-specific case study was found to quantify this, but the mechanism is structurally the same one that killed profitability of prior-patch "easy crafts" (see §4).
- **Mirror is a store of value, not a purchase you make for gear** unless you're already at the very top of the wealth curve — its price tracks (and outpaces) overall Divine inflation, so holding raw Divine while Mirror appreciates is a real opportunity cost. [directional pattern, corroborated across 3 sources in §1]
- **"Rocky Atlas" as a named unique appears to not exist** — if this name came from a stream/video, it was almost certainly a mishearing of Rakiata's Flow or Garukhan's Resolve (both Atlas-anomaly-gated Lineage Support Gems, not accessories). Don't search trade for a literal "Rocky Atlas" item.

## Profit angles

- **Farm Atlas-anomaly boss drops (Rakiata's Flow / Garukhan's Resolve tier):** these are gated by a rare, specific Atlas anomaly spawn (not guaranteed, not craftable) but sell in the tens-to-100+ Divine range per drop and are broadly build-agnostic in demand — high per-drop payout even at low frequency. [§3]
- **Omen of Putrefaction low-investment ring/amulet crafting** (XTheFarmerX-documented): cheap consumable, replaces all mods with up to 6 revealed Desecrated modifiers and auto-corrupts — framed by creators as an accessible, low-capital entry point to profitable crafting rather than a whale-tier mirror craft. [§5]
- **Buy-low-week-1, hold-Divine-denominated-assets strategy:** because Chaos/Exalted inflate faster than Divine early in a league (§1's 78→598 Exalted-per-Divine drift), acquiring Divine-priced assets (uniques, high-end bases) early and holding, rather than sitting on Exalted/Chaos, preserves purchasing power as the league ages.
- **Mirror-service crafting/fulfillment** (supply side): a crafter who can reliably produce a specific mirror-tier item can charge a flat 500–880 Divine service fee per copy on top of the buyer's own Mirror, per the OpenMirror fee data (§2) — recurring income for anyone who nails a demonstrably strong, repeatable mirror-tier recipe (e.g., a proven Fubgun-style Ice Shot bow).
- **Boss-invitation-bulk-buying farms**: buying invitations in bulk and grinding a single boss for its specific high-value unique drop is creator-reported to net ~60 Div/hr "safe" baseline with upside spikes near 186 Div in a lucky hour — a repeatable, capital-scalable strategy for players who can afford to buy invitations in bulk upfront. [§1 table]
- **Vaal Cultivation Orb gambling on non-Vaal uniques**, while high-variance, has a real (if unquantified) chance of converting a cheap unique into something in the Headhunter/chase-unique tier, since it explicitly can produce a random corrupted unique "of the same item class" — a lottery-ticket profit angle for players sitting on a stack of low-value uniques of the right item classes. [§5]
